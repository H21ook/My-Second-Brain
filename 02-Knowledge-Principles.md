# Knowledge Principles

## Purpose

Every knowledge note should increase the long-term value of this Second Brain.

Knowledge is not collected to archive information.  
Knowledge is stored to improve future thinking, decision making, learning, and AI collaboration.

---

# 1. Store Knowledge, Not Information

Do not save information simply because it exists.

Only store information that is likely to be useful again.

Before creating a note, ask:

- Will I need this again?
    
- Will this save future work?
    
- Will this improve future AI responses?
    
- Does it reduce future research?
    

If the answer is **no**, do not store it.

---

# 2. One Idea Per Note

Each note should represent one clear concept.

Avoid mixing unrelated topics.

Small focused notes are easier to:

- reuse
    
- update
    
- reference
    
- link
    
- search
    
- summarize
    

---

# 3. Prefer Evergreen Knowledge

Knowledge should survive time.

Avoid storing temporary facts unless they are historically important.

Store principles, patterns, architectures, lessons, and decisions instead of temporary news.

---

# 4. Distill Before Saving

Never save raw information.

Convert information into understanding.

Every note should answer:

- What is it?
    
- Why does it matter?
    
- When should I use it?
    
- What are the trade-offs?
    
- What should I avoid?
    

---

# 5. Write for Future Me

Assume you will forget everything.

Every note should be understandable without needing the original conversation or source.

Future-you should understand the note in seconds.

---

# 6. AI Must Understand It

Write notes so both humans and AI can understand them.

Avoid unnecessary abbreviations, missing context, or ambiguous wording.

The better the note, the better AI assistance becomes.

---

# 7. Link Knowledge

Knowledge should never exist in isolation.

Whenever possible, connect notes through meaningful relationships.

Examples:

- prerequisite
    
- related concept
    
- alternative
    
- dependency
    
- implementation
    
- best practice
    

A network of knowledge is more valuable than isolated documents.

---

# 8. Prefer Generalization

Store reusable patterns instead of one-time solutions.

Instead of documenting one bug, document the underlying lesson.

Instead of storing one prompt, document the prompting technique.

---

# 9. Keep Knowledge Alive

Knowledge is continuously refined.

Improve notes whenever you discover:

- better explanations
    
- better examples
    
- new best practices
    
- corrections
    
- simpler approaches
    

Do not hesitate to rewrite existing notes.

---

# 10. Reduce Future Token Usage

Knowledge should reduce repeated AI conversations.

A well-written note should allow future AI sessions to understand context with minimal tokens.

Summaries, reusable templates, and structured notes are preferred over long conversations.

---

# 11. Prefer Decisions Over Discussions

Do not store long discussions.

Store the final decision and why it was made.

Include rejected alternatives only if they explain future trade-offs.

---

# 12. Quality Over Quantity

A small collection of excellent notes is more valuable than thousands of low-quality notes.

Every note should earn its place in the Second Brain.
